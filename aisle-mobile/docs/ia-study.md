## Method, and what it cannot tell you

A participant is granted correct knowledge of **what the item is** (its concepts,
from `tools/ia-lexicon.mjs`, which maps words to ideas and never to departments)
and then has to reach it using only the labels in the tree. So what is being
measured is the labels, not the shopper.

- **Vocabulary coverage:** the lexicon characterises 98.9% of items. 6 carry no concept at all, so their trials are effectively coin flips and they depress the score without being real navigation failures.
- **Scoring is strict.** A trial only counts as found if the participant lands on the
  exact aisle. Reaching the right department and scanning from there, which is what
  people actually do, is scored as a miss.
- **Browse only.** Search is the primary way into a 557-item catalogue and is not
  exercised here at all. These numbers are a floor on findability, not a ceiling.
- **Irreducible ambiguity.** Some items are honestly two things at once — frozen
  blueberries are a fruit and a frozen good. Cross-listing (`alsoIn`) resolves many,
  but a residue of disagreement is the correct outcome, not a defect to tune away.

Percentages are useful for comparing one revision of the tree against the next.
They are not a prediction of how real people would score.
